# RETAILPULSE DECISION INTELLIGENCE (DI)
## SPECIFICATION 01: PROBLEM DEFINITION, OPERATIONAL CONSTRAINTS & DATA GENERATION STRATEGY

---

### 1. Business Problem Statement

Retail inventory management operates at the nexus of two opposing financial hazards:
1. **Stockouts & Under-stocking:** Inability to fulfill customer demand results in immediate lost revenue, customer churn, and **demand censoring** (unobserved latent demand).
2. **Over-stocking & Excessive Inventory:** Excessive stock locks up scarce working capital, incurs holding costs (storage, insurance, cost of capital), and increases markdown/spoilage risks.

Most retail enterprises rely on simple historical moving averages or unadjusted sales logs. However, when an item is out of stock ($I_t = 0$), recorded sales are identically zero ($Y_t = 0$), regardless of whether latent customer demand $D_t$ was 10 units or 100 units. A standard predictive model trained on raw sales learns that zero stock implies zero demand, leading to depressed forecasts, smaller replenishment orders, and chronic stockout traps (the **"inventory death spiral"**).

**The Objective:**
Build **RetailPulse DI**, a local, deterministic, and defensible Decision Intelligence platform that:
- Ingests multi-echelon retail transactional data with automated schema validation and dead-letter quarantine.
- Uncensors historical demand using inventory availability indicators.
- Produces calibrated probabilistic demand forecasts ($P_{10}, P_{50}, P_{90}$) over stochastic supplier lead-time horizons.
- Generates constrained optimal purchase orders that satisfy supplier Minimum Order Quantities (MOQs), pack sizes, and executive working capital budgets.
- Reconciles financial and operational metrics with 100% mathematical parity across SQL views, Python analytics, and Power BI DAX.

### 3. Operational Constraints & Boundaries

Replenishment decisions generated by RetailPulse DI must enforce real-world supply chain physical constraints:
1. **Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ):** Suppliers do not accept orders below fixed unit thresholds (e.g., Supplier S1 requires MOQ = 50 units for Product P1).
2. **Pack / Case Multiples:** Items must be ordered in discrete packaging multiples (e.g., case size = 12 units). Any order quantity $Q_i$ must satisfy:
   $$Q_i = k \cdot \text{PackSize}_i, \quad k \in \mathbb{N}_{\ge 0}$$
3. **Supplier Lead Time Uncertainty:** Supplier transit time $L_i$ is not deterministic; it follows a stochastic distribution (e.g., $L_i \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu_{L_i})$) with occasional supplier delays.
4. **Working Capital Budget Ceiling:** Total replenishment spend across all SKUs in a review cycle cannot exceed an executive-mandated budget ceiling $B$:
   $$\sum_{i \in \text{SKUs}} Q_i \cdot \text{CostPrice}_i \le B$$
5. **Non-Negativity & Capacity:** Order quantities cannot be negative, and store stock cannot exceed shelf/backroom capacity.

### 4. Explicit Non-Goals (Scope Defensibility)

To maintain architectural purity, robustness, and suitability for technical interview defense, the following are explicit non-goals:
- **No Heavyweight Distributed Clusters:** No Apache Spark or Kafka clusters. All data processing uses vectorized Pandas/NumPy, embedded SQL (SQLite/DuckDB/SQL Server), and native Python.
- **No Black-Box Opaque Deep Learning:** No uninterpretable deep neural networks with thousands of uncalibrated weights. We utilize glass-box baselines and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (LightGBM) with explicit lag features and quantile loss.
- **No Cloud-Vendor Lock-in or Paid SaaS:** Zero reliance on proprietary AWS/Azure/GCP cloud APIs, external paid LLMs, or third-party SaaS pipelines. The entire system executes on a local development environment.
- **No Intra-day Dynamic Pricing:** Prices are assumed exogenous or promotional schedule-driven; dynamic algorithmic price adjustments are outside the scope of inventory replenishment.

### 5. Demand Censoring & Uncensoring Methodology

#### The Mathematical Problem:
Let $D_{i,s,t}$ be the unobserved customer demand for product $i$ at store $s$ on day $t$.
Let $I_{i,s,t}$ be the available beginning-of-day on-hand inventory.
The observed sales $Y_{i,s,t}$ is a right-censored observation of latent demand:
$$Y_{i,s,t} = \min(D_{i,s,t}, I_{i,s,t})$$

When $I_{i,s,t} = 0$:
$$Y_{i,s,t} = 0 \quad (\text{even if } D_{i,s,t} > 0)$$
$$\text{Lost Sales}_{i,s,t} = D_{i,s,t} - Y_{i,s,t}$$

#### Uncensoring Strategy:
1. **Out-of-Stock Indicator:**
   $$S_{i,s,t} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } I_{i,s,t} = 0 \text{ or } (I_{i,s,t} \le \text{threshold and } Y_{i,s,t} = I_{i,s,t}) \\ 0 & \text{otherwise (in-stock)} \end{cases}$$
2. **Heuristic Rate-of-Sale Imputation:**
   For days where $S_{i,s,t} = 1$, estimate latent demand $\hat{D}_{i,s,t}$ using the localized un-censored moving average:
   $$\hat{D}_{i,s,t} = \text{RollingMean}(Y_{i,s, \tau} \mid S_{i,s,\tau} = 0, \tau \in [t-28, t-1])$$
   adjusted by day-of-week seasonality factors.
3. **Comparative Evaluation:** In Phase 5, the model suite will explicitly train and evaluate forecasts with raw sales $Y$ vs. uncensored demand $\hat{D}$ to quantify the exact uplift in forecast accuracy and revenue recovery.

### 6. Synthetic Retail Data Generation Specification

To rigorously test our ETL, ML, and Optimization pipelines, the generator will produce a calibrated, synthetic multi-echelon retail dataset:

#### A. Dimensions & Scale
- **Time Horizon:** 730 days (2 full calendar years, e.g., 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-31).
- **Stores:** 3 stores with distinct customer demographics:
  - `STR01`: Flagship Urban Store (high traffic, small backroom).
  - `STR02`: Suburban Superstore (medium-high volume, large storage).
  - `STR03`: Express Neighborhood Store (convenience format, high volatility).
- **Products:** 50 SKUs across 5 merchandise categories:
  - *Perishables / Fresh:* High velocity, short shelf life, low margin.
  - *Ambient / Packaged Grocery:* Medium velocity, steady demand, standard margin.
  - *Beverages / Snacks:* High weekend seasonality, promotion-sensitive.
  - *Electronics / Accessories:* Low velocity, intermittent/lumpy demand, high margin, high holding cost.
  - *Household / Cleaning:* Steady, predictable demand, high bulk/case sizes.
- **Suppliers:** 10 regional and national suppliers with distinct lead times (2 to 14 days), lead time variance ($\sigma_L$), pack sizes (6, 12, 24 units), and MOQs.

#### B. Demand Dynamics Simulation
For each product $i$, store $s$, and day $t$:
1. **Base Rate:** $\lambda_{0,i,s}$ assigned according to SKU velocity profile.
2. **Day-of-Week Effect:** $\gamma_{\text{dow}} \in [0.8, 1.4]$ (Saturday/Sunday peaks).
3. **Annual Seasonality & Trends:** Sinusoidal seasonal wave + holiday spikes (e.g., Thanksgiving/Christmas/Black Friday).
4. **Promotional Calendar:** Randomized scheduled promotions giving a temporary multiplier $\gamma_{\text{promo}} \in [1.2, 2.5]$.
5. **Arrival Process:**
   - Fast-moving SKUs: Demand sampled from $D_{i,s,t} \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_{i,s,t})$.
   - Intermittent / Slow-moving SKUs: Demand sampled from a Zero-Inflated Poisson or Negative Binomial distribution to simulate high proportions of zero-demand days with occasional bulk purchases.

#### C. Inventory State Simulation (Physical Reality Engine)
Each day progresses sequentially:
1. **Arrival of In-Transit Orders:** Orders due on day $t$ are received:
   $$I_{\text{start}, t} = I_{\text{end}, t-1} + \sum \text{DeliveredOrders}_t$$
2. **Customer Demand Realization:**
   $$Y_t = \min(I_{\text{start}, t}, D_t)$$
   $$\text{LostSales}_t = \max(0, D_t - Y_t)$$
   $$I_{\text{end}, t} = I_{\text{start}, t} - Y_t$$
3. **Stockout Triggering:** Deliberate historical periods of delayed supplier fulfillment or stock rationing to produce realistic censored sales periods ($I_t = 0$).

#### D. Controlled Data Corruptions (Quarantine Test Suite)
To stress-test the Pydantic and SQL dead-letter ingestion quarantine (Phase 3), the synthetic generator will inject controlled anomalies into a designated test batch:
- **Negative Prices:** E.g., `unit_price = -15.50` or `cost_price = -8.00`.
- **Orphaned Foreign Keys:** Sales records referencing `product_id = "PRD999"` or `store_id = "STR999"` not present in dimension tables.
- **Invalid Dates:** Sales timestamps occurring in the future or formatted illegally.
- **Negative Inventory / Quantities:** Return records disguised as sales or negative ending inventory.
- **Duplicate Primary Keys:** Multiple transaction logs sharing identical composite keys (`date_key`, `store_id`, `product_id`).

### 7. Business Metrics & Reconciliation Mathematical Proofs

All metrics must produce identical figures across SQL, Python, and DAX:

1. **Net Sales:**
   $$\text{Net Sales} = \sum (\text{Quantity Sold} \times \text{Unit Selling Price}) - \text{Discounts}$$
2. **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):**
   $$\text{COGS} = \sum (\text{Quantity Sold} \times \text{Unit Cost Price})$$
3. **Gross Profit:**
   $$\text{Gross Profit} = \text{Net Sales} - \text{COGS}$$
4. **Gross Margin %:**
   $$\text{Gross Margin \%} = \left( \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \right) \times 100$$
5. **Days of Supply (DoS):**
   $$\text{Average Daily Sales (ADS)}_{30} = \frac{1}{30} \sum_{\tau = t-29}^{t} Y_\tau$$
   $$\text{DoS}_t = \frac{I_{\text{end}, t}}{\text{ADS}_{30}}$$
6. **Stockout Rate %:**
   $$\text{Stockout Rate \%} = \left( \frac{\sum_{t=1}^N \mathbb{I}(I_t = 0)}{N} \right) \times 100$$
7. **Inventory Turnover:**
   $$\text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{365} \text{COGS}_t}{\frac{1}{365} \sum_{t=1}^{365} (I_{\text{end}, t} \times \text{Unit Cost Price})}$$
